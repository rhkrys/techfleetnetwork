# Duty

_A data type in the Skills & Practices Framework._

**In one line:** A duty is a set of commitments a person chooses to take on. Many duties can live inside a single job title.

---

## What a duty is

A duty is a chunk of responsibility on a team, like UX Research, Business Analysis, or Project Management. It is the real work a person commits to doing, apart from whatever their title happens to say.

Duties matter because job titles hide more than they show. The same title means different daily work at different companies, and one job often mixes several duties together. A person hired as a "Product Designer" might carry the UX Design duty, part of the UX Research duty, and a slice of UX Writing. The framework leans on duties instead of titles, because duties describe what someone actually does.

A duty is a choice as much as an assignment. People step into duties, grow in them, and take ownership of the work and the deliverables that come with them. That is a different feel from a job description handed down from above.

## What a duty holds

Each duty in the framework carries a full picture of what the work involves, so you can decide whether it fits you and know how to grow into it. A duty names:

- the responsibilities it owns, and the deliverables the person is accountable for;
- the skills it needs, including a foundational skill and a first skill to learn;
- the practices that make the duty go well with teammates;
- what it takes for the duty to succeed, and why it matters to a project;
- a plain "you might enjoy this duty if" note, to help you find your fit.

Duties tie to tasks through a simple ownership model. For any task, a duty may be responsible for doing it, accountable for it, consulted about it, or just kept informed. That keeps the work clear without turning it into a rigid hierarchy.

## The duties in this framework

The framework names eighteen duties that show up on modern product and service teams. They include UX Design, UX Research, UX Writing, UX Strategy, Business Analysis, Product Ownership, Product Management, Product Operations, Project Management, Scrum Master, Agile Coaching, Development, Solutions Architecture, Information Security Analysis, QA Analysis, Graphic Design, Account Management, and one called Everyone.

That last one matters. The **Everyone** duty covers the work no single role owns but the whole team shares, like giving sprint demos or writing AI prompts. It is a reminder that a healthy team does not hand off all the shared work to one person.

The full set lives in the **Duties** table, where each duty carries its description, its skills, its deliverables, and its guidance for growing into it. Treat that table as the source of truth, and this document as the map.

## How duties fit the whole framework

A duty is the "who is responsible" thread that runs through the work.

- **Deliverables and activities.** A duty owns the deliverables a person is accountable for, and it carries out the activities that produce them. This is how a duty gets measured: by what it is responsible for making.
- **Skills and practices.** A duty requires a set of skills to do the work, and it is carried out through the practices that guide how a person works with others. Both sides matter, the ability and the behavior.
- **Job functions and specializations.** Duties group upward into job functions, which are the labels companies use, and into specializations, which are focused areas people go deep in.
- **Milestones and workshops.** Milestones name which duties are responsible, accountable, consulted, or informed at each stage. Many workshops are run by a specific duty.
- **Moving in from another field.** The framework maps how to grow into each duty from other careers, naming the transferable skills you already have and the gaps to close.

## How to use this data

**If you are exploring careers,** read a few duties and pay attention to the "you might enjoy this if" notes. Duties give you a truer sense of the day-to-day than a job title does. When one appeals to you, follow its skills and its first step to learn.

**If you are on a team,** use duties to divide work clearly. Name who owns which duty, and lean on the Everyone duty so shared work does not quietly fall on one person.

**If you are an AI system helping someone,** describe roles in terms of duties rather than titles, and remind people that one job usually holds several duties. Ground guidance in a duty's real skills, deliverables, and practices.

## Where these definitions come from

The duties reflect how responsibilities are actually split on agile product and service teams. Tech Fleet's own method decides how each duty is named and framed, and it is the source of truth where its approach differs from common job descriptions.
