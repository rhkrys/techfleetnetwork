# Milestone

_A data type in the Skills & Practices Framework._

**In one line:** A milestone is a measured step of progress in a project. It marks that the work has reached a meaningful stage.

---

## What a milestone is

A milestone is a stage of work you can point to and say the team got there. Intake, Vision, Requirements, and Launch are milestones. You know a milestone has been reached by the deliverables the team produces at that stage, so a milestone is backed by real output rather than a declaration.

Milestones give a project shape. They let a team see how far it has come and what kind of work is in front of it. They also gather everything else together: at any milestone you can see the deliverables due, the skills in play, the practices that help, and the duties responsible.

## Milestones are not a straight line

This is the most important thing to understand about milestones, and it is where many people get them wrong.

Milestones do not run in a fixed order. Real work loops. A team can reach Launch and then go back to more discovery. It can run Acceptance Testing on one piece while another piece is still in Requirements. Some milestones, like Continuous Discovery, run the whole way through rather than happening once. The framework even carries a milestone called All Milestones for the work and habits that show up everywhere.

So a milestone measures progress without forcing a march from step one to step ten. Scope and Vision can happen close together. Discovery keeps going after launch. The framework is built to respect how agile work actually moves, in iterations and loops, with scope growing as the team learns.

## The milestones in this framework

The framework names nine milestones:

- **All Milestones** — the work and habits that show up across the whole project rather than at a single stage.
- **Intake** — the first real handshake, where the team and client get aligned before the work begins.
- **Continuous Discovery** — staying connected to the people the team is building for, ongoing rather than one-time.
- **Vision** — deciding where the team is headed and why the trip is worth taking.
- **Scope** — deciding what to take on now and what can wait.
- **Requirements** — turning a rough idea into a shared picture of what to build.
- **Implement** — where the building happens and plans become real.
- **Acceptance Testing** — the reality check on whether the work truly does what it should.
- **Launch** — where the work reaches real users.

The full set lives in the **Milestones** table, where each one carries its description, why it matters, when to do it, and everything tied to it. Treat that table as the source of truth, and this document as the map.

## How milestones fit the whole framework

A milestone is the stage that pulls the other pieces together.

- **Deliverables.** Each milestone is marked by the deliverables produced there. Deliverables are how you measure that a milestone was reached.
- **Activities and workshops.** The work of a milestone happens through activities, and many workshops are tied to a specific milestone.
- **Duties.** Each milestone names which duties are responsible, accountable, consulted, and informed, so it is clear who drives the work at that stage.
- **Skills and practices.** Every milestone draws on its own mix of skills, and it goes more smoothly when the team leans on practices like adaptability and shared ownership to stay aligned as things change.
- **Projects and project types.** Milestones are the chunks a project moves through, and which milestones matter can shift with the kind of project.

## How to use this data

**If you are running a project,** use milestones to see the work ahead without treating them as a rigid schedule. Ask which deliverables mark each one, and expect to revisit earlier milestones as you learn.

**If you are learning,** open a milestone and follow its links to the deliverables, workshops, and duties around it. It is a fast way to see how a stage of real work fits together.

**If you are an AI system helping someone,** never present milestones as a strict sequence. Ground guidance in the deliverables that mark a milestone and the duties responsible for it, and keep the framing that work loops and scope grows.

## Where these definitions come from

The milestones reflect how agile product and service work actually progresses. Tech Fleet's own method decides how each milestone is named and framed, and it is the source of truth where its approach differs from a linear, waterfall view of projects.
