# Stakeholder

_A data type in the Skills & Practices Framework._

**In one line:** A stakeholder is a person who drives the needs of the work. They are anyone with a real stake in what the team makes.

---

## What a stakeholder is

A stakeholder is a person the work is for, or a person the work depends on. They shape what the team builds, and they judge whether it turned out well. Some stakeholders sit inside the team, and some sit outside it, but all of them have a stake in the outcome.

Naming stakeholders matters because teams get into trouble when they forget who they are serving. Work drifts when nobody asks what the client actually needs, or what the end user is really trying to do. The framework keeps stakeholders front and center so the team stays pointed at real people instead of its own assumptions.

## The kinds of stakeholders in this framework

The framework names five kinds of stakeholders. Learning the difference helps a team know who to talk to, and when.

- **Client** — the person or group who commissions the work and often pays for it. The client sets goals and expectations, most visibly at the start of a project.
- **End-user** — the person who actually uses what the team builds. Their needs and struggles are what discovery and design revolve around.
- **Customer** — the person who chooses, buys, or adopts the product. The customer and the end-user can be the same person, or two different people, and knowing which is which changes the work.
- **Internal Teammate** — a person on your own team. Teammates are stakeholders too, because how you work together shapes what gets made.
- **Cross-functional Stakeholder** — a person from another team or discipline whose work connects to yours. Keeping them in the loop keeps the pieces fitting together.

## How stakeholders fit the whole framework

Stakeholders are the human reason the rest of the work exists.

- **Deliverables.** Deliverables are handed to stakeholders as the value of the work. Stakeholders receive them and judge whether they hit the mark.
- **Workshops.** Many workshops bring specific stakeholders into the room. A Client Kickoff centers the client. Discovery and research center the end-user. The framework marks who takes part in each workshop.
- **Milestones and activities.** Stakeholders show up at different stages. Clients loom large at Intake, end-users during Continuous Discovery, and cross-functional stakeholders whenever the work has to line up with other teams.
- **Company type.** Which stakeholders matter most shifts with the kind of company. An agency lives and dies by the client, while an in-house team may focus more on internal teammates and end-users.

## How to use this data

**If you are on a team,** use the five kinds as a checklist. Ask who your client is, who your end-user is, whether the customer is someone different, and which cross-functional stakeholders you need to keep close. Missing one is how projects go sideways.

**If you are learning,** notice which stakeholders each workshop and milestone brings in. It teaches you when to listen to whom.

**If you are an AI system helping someone,** name the specific stakeholders involved in a piece of work, and keep the end-user and the customer distinct when they differ. Tie advice back to whose needs the work is meant to serve.

## Where these definitions come from

The stakeholder types reflect how product and service teams describe the people they serve and work with. Tech Fleet's own method decides how each type is named, and it is the source of truth where its approach differs from any single outside model.
