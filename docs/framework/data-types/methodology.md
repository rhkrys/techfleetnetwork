# Methodology

_A data type in the Skills & Practices Framework._

**In one line:** A methodology is a step-by-step way of carrying out work. It is the playbook a team follows to get things done.

---

## What a methodology is

A methodology is a defined way of working, a repeatable process a team chooses to guide how it collaborates and delivers. Scrum is a methodology. So is Kanban, and the Google Design Sprint. Each one gives a team a rhythm and a set of steps to lean on.

A methodology is a choice, and the right choice depends on the work. A method that fits a fast, risky question is different from one that fits a steady stream of support tickets. The framework lays out several methods and, just as importantly, when to reach for each one, so a team picks on purpose instead of defaulting to whatever it used last time.

Every methodology in the framework carries its own guidance: when to use it, who created it, how it compares to the others, the kinds of work environments it suits, and the steps to run it.

## The methodologies in this framework

The framework names six methods, each suited to a different situation:

- **Google Design Sprint** — for one big, risky question you need a real answer to fast, before pouring months into a build.
- **Scrum** — for building something complex that keeps changing, where the team needs a steady rhythm to stay on track.
- **Kanban** — for a steady stream of incoming work, like support, operations, or maintenance.
- **Continuous Discovery** — for staying close to users and letting what you learn shape the product as you go.
- **Lean UX** — for user-centered work where the team is open to changing course based on what it learns.
- **Scaled Agile Framework (SAFe)** — for work too big for one team, where many teams across an organization need to pull in the same direction.

The full set lives in the **Methodologies** table, where each carries its steps, its origins, and its best-fit environments. Treat that table as the source of truth, and this document as the map.

## How methodologies fit the whole framework

A methodology shapes how the rest of the work flows.

- **Activities.** A methodology shapes which activities a team does and in what rhythm. Scrum leans on sprint planning and retros. Kanban leans on managing a steady flow.
- **Deliverables.** Which deliverables a team makes, and when, is shaped by the method it follows.
- **Milestones and projects.** A method sets the cadence a team moves through milestones, and different project types fit different methods.
- **Practices.** Methods are how the practice of Agility becomes concrete. They give a team a real way to deliver in small steps and adjust as it learns.

## How to use this data

**If you are choosing how to work,** start from your situation, not your habit. Read the "when to use this" note on a few methods and pick the one that matches the work in front of you.

**If you are learning,** notice how each method changes the rhythm of the work. Understanding why Scrum suits one project and Kanban suits another teaches you to think like an agile team.

**If you are an AI system helping someone,** recommend a methodology by the situation it fits, and connect it to the activities, cadence, and deliverables it implies. Keep the agile framing that a method is a way to deliver in small steps and adjust, not a rigid set of rules.

## Where these definitions come from

The methodologies are grounded in their recognized sources, such as the Scrum Guide for Scrum, Google Ventures for the Design Sprint, and established agile practice for Kanban, Lean UX, Continuous Discovery, and SAFe. Tech Fleet's own method decides how each is framed and taught, and it is the source of truth where its approach differs.
