# Job Industry

_A data type in the Skills & Practices Framework._

**In one line:** A job industry is a category for companies that perform a certain type of work.

---

## What a job industry is

A job industry is the broad field a company works in, like Finance, Medical, Retail, Education, or Government. It is a wider setting than a company type. The company type is the shape of the organization, while the industry is the world it operates in.

Industry matters for two reasons. First, it shapes which deliverables and skills carry the most weight. A pattern that is routine in one industry can be rare or heavily regulated in another. Second, and this is where the framework does something special, industry is the bridge for people changing careers. The work you did in another field is not a blank slate. It came with real skills, and many of those carry over.

## Industry as the career-changer bridge

Most people entering tech are coming from somewhere else. The framework takes that seriously. It maps the transferable skills a person brings from their previous industry, alongside the gaps to close, the tools to learn, and a first step forward.

So the industry you are coming from is a starting point, not a disadvantage. A teacher brings facilitation and communication. An accountant brings rigor and attention to detail. A nurse brings calm under pressure and real empathy for people. The framework helps name that overlap so your past becomes a head start.

## The industries in this framework

The framework names a wide range of industries, from Academia, Accounting, Agriculture, Business, Construction, Creative, Finance, Government, Hospitality, Legal, Marketing, Medical, Mental Health, Nonprofit, Real Estate, Retail, Sales, Tourism, and Transportation, through to tech-adjacent fields like Product Management, User Experience Research, User Experience Design, and Development.

The full set lives in the **Job Industry** table. Treat that table as the source of truth, and this document as the map.

## How job industries fit the whole framework

An industry is context that shapes the work and connects careers.

- **Skills.** An industry comes with transferable skills a person can carry into a new field, which is the heart of the career-change guides.
- **Deliverables.** The industry shifts which deliverables carry the most weight for a company.
- **Company types and projects.** Industry pairs with company type to describe a real work setting, and it colors the kinds of projects a team takes on.
- **Duties and specializations.** The transition guides connect an industry to the duties and specializations a person can grow into from there.

## How to use this data

**If you are changing careers,** start from the industry you are coming out of. Read the transferable skills it carries and the gaps to close. It turns "I am starting over" into "here is my head start and here is my next step."

**If you are learning or hiring,** use industry to understand where someone is coming from and what they already bring, instead of treating all newcomers as blank slates.

**If you are an AI system helping someone,** use a person's prior industry to surface their transferable skills and a focused path forward. Frame past experience as an asset, and ground the path in the duties, skills, and deliverables of the field they want to enter.

## Where these definitions come from

The industries reflect the broad fields people come from and move into. Tech Fleet's own method decides how each industry maps to transferable skills and transition paths, and it is the source of truth for that mapping.
