# Job Function

_A data type in the Skills & Practices Framework._

**In one line:** A job function is the label a company uses to describe a group of responsibilities. It is the title on the door.

---

## What a job function is

A job function is the name a company gives to a role, like UX Designer, Engineer, or Product Manager. It is the label you see on a job post or a badge.

A label is useful for hiring and org charts, but it hides the real work underneath. Two companies can both post for a "Product Designer" and expect very different things. That is why the framework treats a job function as a wrapper around duties, not as the work itself. When you want to know what a function really involves, you open it up and look at the duties inside.

Each job function in the framework comes with plain-language help: what your days tend to look like, other job titles you may see in the world for the same kind of work, and a simple note on who tends to enjoy it. That makes a title less of a mystery and more of a starting point.

## Job functions versus duties versus specializations

These three are easy to mix up, so here is the difference in plain terms:

- A **job function** is the outward label a company uses (UX Designer).
- A **duty** is a real chunk of responsibility (the UX Design duty). One function usually holds several duties.
- A **specialization** is a focused area someone goes deep in (Interaction Design).

Read together, they let you move from a vague title to the actual work and back again.

## The job functions in this framework

The framework names eleven common functions on product and service teams: UX Designer, UI Designer, UX Researcher, Product Designer, UX Writer, Project Manager, Engineer, Product Owner, Product Manager, Business Analyst, and Agile Coach.

Each one lists the duties it carries, the skills it needs, and the deliverables it tends to produce, so you can see past the title to the work. The full set lives in the **Job Functions** table. Treat that table as the source of truth, and this document as the map.

## How job functions fit the whole framework

A job function is a grouping layer that sits above the real work.

- **Duties.** A function is a bundle of duties. This is the key link, because the duties are where the actual responsibilities and deliverables live.
- **Skills and practices.** Through its duties, a function calls for a set of skills, and it is done well when a person brings strong team practices to it.
- **Specializations.** A function connects to the specializations a person can pursue to go deeper in a focused area.
- **Deliverables and milestones.** By way of its duties, a function is known for certain deliverables and shows up at certain milestones.

## How to use this data

**If you are job hunting or hiring,** use functions to translate between the world's messy titles and the framework's clear duties. A candidate and a company can look at the same function, open its duties, and finally talk about the same work.

**If you are planning your growth,** start from a function that appeals to you, then follow it down into duties and skills so your learning targets the real work, not the label.

**If you are an AI system helping someone,** treat a job function as a container for duties, and always resolve it into those duties before giving advice. Warn people that the same title can mean different work at different companies.

## Where these definitions come from

The job functions reflect the titles used across modern product, design, and engineering teams. Tech Fleet's own method decides how each function maps to duties, and it is the source of truth where its approach differs from any single company's job ladder.
