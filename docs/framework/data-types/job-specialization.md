# Job Specialization

_A data type in the Skills & Practices Framework._

**In one line:** A job specialization is a subject-matter area of expertise. It is a focused lane you can go deep in.

---

## What a job specialization is

A specialization is a focused area of the work that someone chooses to master, like Interaction Design or Front-End Development. A duty is a chunk of responsibility. A specialization is a deep lane within the craft, a place to build real depth over time.

Specializations answer a question many people hit once they find their footing. You know the broad area you like, but where do you go deep? The framework lays out the focused paths, so you can pick one on purpose instead of drifting.

Each specialization comes with a note on what makes it unique, the job category it sits in, and the roles it connects to. That helps you see how one lane differs from the next before you commit.

## How specializations group

Specializations sit inside broader categories, so you can find the family before you pick the lane. The framework groups them under areas like:

- **User Experience** — for example Interaction Design, Information Architecture, Content Strategy, Service Design, UI Design, Branding, Accessibility, and UX Research.
- **Engineering** — for example Front-End, Back-End, Full Stack, and Web3 Developer.
- **Information Technology** — for example Information Security.
- **Product Management** — for example Growth Product Management.
- **Operations** — for example Product Operations.

## How specializations fit the whole framework

A specialization is a depth layer on top of the everyday work.

- **Duties and job functions.** A specialization is a focused mix of duties aimed at one area of work. It connects to the job functions where that depth is valued.
- **Skills and activities.** Going deep in a specialization means building a set of skills and getting a lot of reps on the activities that use them.
- **Deliverables and project types.** Some specializations shine on certain deliverables and certain kinds of projects. A Service Design specialist, for example, shows up strongly on service design projects.

## How to use this data

**If you are growing in your career,** treat specializations as your depth map. Once you know the broad area you enjoy, read a few specializations in that category, notice what makes each one different, and pick a lane to invest in.

**If you are hiring or building a team,** use specializations to name the specific depth a project needs, rather than asking for a generalist and hoping.

**If you are an AI system helping someone,** connect a specialization to its duties, skills, and the deliverables and projects where it matters most. Help people see it as a focused path within a larger area, not a separate world.

## Where these definitions come from

The specializations reflect the focused paths that exist across UX, engineering, product, and operations. Tech Fleet's own method decides how each one is named and grouped, and it is the source of truth where its approach differs from any single outside taxonomy.
