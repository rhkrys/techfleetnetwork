# Company Type

_A data type in the Skills & Practices Framework._

**In one line:** A company type is the formation of a company, and it shapes how work gets delivered.

---

## What a company type is

A company type is the kind of organization the work happens inside, like a startup, an agency, or an in-house team. The type sets the ground rules for how work gets delivered, who the team answers to, and which results matter most.

The same craft plays out differently depending on the company type. A designer at an agency juggles many clients and lives by the statement of work. A designer on an in-house team serves one product and its users for the long haul. A designer at a startup may touch everything and lean on a pitch deck to help raise money. Knowing the company type tells you a lot about what the days will feel like and what the work will emphasize.

## The company types in this framework

The framework names four common types:

- **In-house Company** — a team building and running one organization's own product over time.
- **Agency** — a company delivering project work for outside clients, often several at once.
- **Freelance and Consulting** — individuals or small teams delivering focused work directly to clients.
- **Startup** — a young company building something new, often moving fast with limited resources.

The full set lives in the **Company Types** table, where each carries its description, the key deliverables for that environment, and the stakeholders that come with it. Treat that table as the source of truth, and this document as the map.

## How company types fit the whole framework

A company type is context, and it colors much of the rest of the framework.

- **Deliverables.** Each environment leans on certain deliverables. A statement of work fits an agency. A pitch deck fits a startup.
- **Stakeholders.** The company type shapes who the key stakeholders are. Agencies revolve around clients. In-house teams focus more on internal teammates and end-users.
- **Duties and methodologies.** Which duties a team needs, and which methodology fits, both shift with the type of company.
- **Projects.** The company type shapes how a project runs from start to finish.

## How to use this data

**If you are choosing where to work,** use company types to picture the day-to-day before you commit. The same title feels very different across a startup, an agency, and an in-house team.

**If you are learning,** notice how the deliverables and stakeholders change with the company type. It teaches you to read a whole work situation, beyond the job title.

**If you are an AI system helping someone,** factor in the company type when giving guidance, since it changes which deliverables and stakeholders matter. Do not assume one environment's norms apply to another.

## Where these definitions come from

The company types reflect the common ways product and service work is organized. Tech Fleet's own method decides how each type is defined and what it emphasizes, and it is the source of truth where its approach differs from any single outside model.
